01

What Impressed Me

AI is actually being used, not just talked about

One of the first things I noticed is that everyone on the team actively uses AI to solve problems, visualize ideas, and write documentation. It is not a policy or a slide in a deck — it is just how people work here. That is genuinely motivating and sets a high bar I am excited to meet.

Knowledge Agent — vibe-coded and shipped

The Knowledge Agent feature caught my attention immediately. A fully vibe-coded feature that made it into the product is a strong signal of what is possible here. It shows the team has both the technical ambition and the trust to experiment boldly — and that is exciting from a product and design perspective.

The depth of content customization

Coming from a background where clients constantly push for more control, I was impressed by the level of customization in the content editor — custom icons, custom steps, flexible layouts. It feels like the product was built with endless possibilities in mind rather than a minimum viable editor. That is a real differentiator.

03

UI Observations

FRICTION "Clear" action hidden inside the input field

On first encounter, the "clear" arrow felt out of place. Two issues compound: the button disappears the moment the user starts typing, making it nearly impossible to discover. And placing a session-reset action inside a conversational input field is a context mismatch — the input's affordance is for composing messages, not triggering session-level actions.

[ Add screenshot ]
QUESTION No error recovery on a binary action

There is a single-tap binary action with no way to undo or recover. This may be intentional — but if it is not, it is worth revisiting, especially for first-time users who may tap it accidentally.

[ Add screenshot ]
NOTE Drag handle placement in widget home view

My first instinct was that the drag handle and overflow menu should swap sides. On reflection, it is consistent with the floating chat state where drag handle and close are already grouped together — so it is a pattern carry-through rather than an inconsistency. Worth a conversation, but not a clear issue.

[ Add screenshot ]
FRICTION KB vs. Content tab distinction unclear

As a newcomer, I had trouble distinguishing between the KB tab and the Content tab — they are visually very similar. This may be a familiarity issue rather than a structural problem, but worth noting as something a new user or customer might also stumble on.

[ Add screenshot ]
FRICTION Three different navigation patterns in Settings

Across settings screens there are three distinct ways to navigate back or exit: Save & Cancel, Save & Back, and a Back arrow — each in a different position. I found myself clicking the X mark and having to re-enter settings. In Rights Management there is no way to navigate back without closing the panel entirely or saving a change first.

04

Product Improvement

Keyboard arrow navigation in guides

When previewing long guides, my natural instinct was to use keyboard arrow keys to navigate between steps — but the product does not currently support this. I later found a customer (Tilt) mentioning the same need in a Gong call, which suggests it is not just my expectation. Could be a low-effort addition with high perceived polish.

05

Looking Ahead

More shadowing across teams

I would love to observe how processes and tasks flow in Engineering and Customer Success in particular. Seeing the product through different lenses would sharpen my observations.

Smoother test environment access

The current process of requesting permissions and deploying a test environment each time adds friction to exploration. Understanding how to deploy vibe-coded solutions directly to the test environment — the way Piotr does it — would meaningfully speed up my workflow.
